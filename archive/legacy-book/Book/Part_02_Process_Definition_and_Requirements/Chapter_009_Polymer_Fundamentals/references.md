# Chapter 9 — Source Register

## Standards and primary normative sources

1. **ISO 9080:2012** — *Plastics piping and ducting systems — Determination of the long-term hydrostatic strength of thermoplastics materials in pipe form by extrapolation.*  
   Use: Basis for statements concerning statistical extrapolation of long-term hydrostatic strength.  
   Status checked: Published; confirmed current by ISO.  
   Official record: https://www.iso.org/standard/43860.html

2. **ISO 12162:2009** — *Thermoplastics materials for pipes and fittings for pressure applications — Classification, designation and design coefficient.*  
   Use: MRS-based classification, material designation, and method for calculating design stress.  
   Official record: https://www.iso.org/standard/43865.html

3. **ISO 15494:2015 + Amendment 1:2020** — *Plastics piping systems for industrial applications — PB, PE, PE-RT, PE-X and PP — Metric series for specifications for components and the system.*  
   Use: Industrial-system context, component/system scope, and requirement that components withstand expected mechanical, thermal, and chemical demands.  
   Current-edition caution: ISO lists Edition 3 as FDIS and expected to replace the 2015 edition. The governing project edition must be checked at time of use.  
   Official records:  
   https://www.iso.org/standard/55627.html  
   https://www.iso.org/standard/78114.html

4. **ISO/FDIS 15494, Edition 3** — Final draft under approval in 2026.  
   Use: Standards-watch item only; not cited as a published mandatory standard.  
   Official record: https://www.iso.org/standard/86634.html

## Industry quality-assurance sources

5. **PE100+ Association — PE100+ Quality Materials List and test requirements.**  
   Use: Distinguishing PE100+ quality-list requirements from the ISO MRS material class; monitoring of creep rupture strength, stress-crack resistance, and rapid-crack-propagation resistance.  
   Source: https://www.pe100plus.com/PE-Pipes/materials/Quality-Material-lists/PE-100-Quality-Materials-List/high-quality-i239.html

6. **PE100+ Association — Meaning of PE80, PE100, and PE100-RC designations.**  
   Use: Supporting explanation that PE80 and PE100 correspond to MRS classes of 8 MPa and 10 MPa respectively, based on ISO 9080/ISO 12162 framework.  
   Source: https://www.pe100plus.com/PE-Pipes/Technical-guidance/model/Materials/mrs/r1046.html

7. **PE100+ Association — Test Requirements.**  
   Use: Clarifying that listed compounds first conform to relevant system standards and are then subjected to additional independent recurring tests.  
   Source: https://www.pe100plus.com/PE-Pipes/materials/Test-Requirements/requirements-i234.html

8. **PE100+ Association — PE100-RC+ qualification announcement and test framework.**  
   Use: Current industry context for enhanced slow-crack-growth testing.  
   Source: https://www.pe100plus.com/PE-Pipes/materials/Quality-Material-lists/PE-100-RC-Quality-Materials-List/PE-100-RC-Quality-Materials-List-i3351.html

## Foundational textbooks for full editorial review

9. Brydson, J. A. — *Plastics Materials.*  
   Intended use: Polymer structure, amorphous/semi-crystalline behaviour, additives, and processing effects.

10. Osswald, T. A., and Menges, G. — *Materials Science of Polymers for Engineers.*  
    Intended use: Viscoelasticity, glass transition, crystallinity, creep, and processing–structure–property relationships.

11. Ward, I. M., and Sweeney, J. — *Mechanical Properties of Solid Polymers.*  
    Intended use: Time-dependent mechanical behaviour and fracture fundamentals.

12. Plastics Pipe Institute — *Handbook of Polyethylene Pipe.*  
    Intended use: PE pressure-pipe materials, long-term strength, joining context, and industry practice.

## Evidence notes

- The current chapter uses official ISO abstracts and industry quality-assurance records for standards-status and classification claims.
- Detailed numerical property values are intentionally excluded from this fundamentals chapter unless tied to a specific material, standard, temperature, and test method.
- Manufacturer-specific property claims are deferred to material-family chapters and must not be generalized without independent support.
- `PE100+` is treated as an industry quality designation and not as a standardized strength class above PE100.
