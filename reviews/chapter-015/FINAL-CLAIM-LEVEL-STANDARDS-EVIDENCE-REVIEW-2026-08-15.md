## 2. Authoritative terminology / classification sources

### S015-008 — current basic polymerization classification

**PASS.**

The official IUPAC recommendation **Basic classification and definitions of polymerization reactions (IUPAC Recommendations 2025)** is published in *Pure and Applied Chemistry* 98(7) (2026), 1105–1117, DOI `10.1515/pac-2025-0490`.

Chapter 015 correctly uses its hierarchy:

- step polymerization;
  - additive step polymerization = polyaddition;
  - condensative step polymerization = polycondensation;
- chain polymerization;
  - additive chain polymerization;
  - condensative chain polymerization.

The historical `addition polymerization / condensation polymerization` pair is retained only as ambiguous/legacy language and not as the controlling taxonomy.

### S015-004 — current chain-polymerization terminology

**PASS.**

The official IUPAC Recommendation **Terminology for chain polymerization** (Recommendations 2021, published 2022 in *Pure and Applied Chemistry* 94, 1093–1147) remains the controlling modern source for chain-carrier, coordination-polymerization and related mechanism terminology used in the chapter.

### S015-001 — IUPAC Gold Book

**PASS WITH PUBLICATION-TIME VERSION RECHECK.**

Current Gold Book citations identify the *Compendium of Chemical Terminology*, 5th ed. (2025), online version 5.0.0. Individual load-bearing term IDs recorded in `references.md` are appropriate for the chapter's terminology layer.

Because the online Gold Book is continuously reviewed/updated, the current version/access date shall be rechecked at publication freeze.

### S015-006 — ISO 472:2013 + Amd 1:2018

**PASS FOR CURRENT VOCABULARY ROLE; LIFECYCLE HOLD.**

The official ISO record remains Published at stage `90.92 — International Standard to be revised`; Amendment 1:2018 remains published.

Chapter 015 uses ISO 472 only as a plastics-vocabulary cross-reference, not as polymerization mechanism or design authority.

Publication-time lifecycle recheck remains mandatory.

### S015-007 — ISO 1043-1:2011 + Amd 1:2016

**PASS FOR CURRENT ABBREVIATION ROLE; LIFECYCLE HOLD.**

The official ISO record remains Published/Confirmed at stage `90.93`; Amendment 1:2016 remains published.

Chapter 015 uses the standard only for polymer symbols/abbreviations where applicable.

## 3. Primary research claim audit — S015-009 through S015-016

### S015-009 — Piovano et al. 2018

**PASS.**

Retained use is limited to the studied MgCl2/TiCl4/AlEt3 catalyst-genesis/activation system and the conclusion that catalyst preparation/activation provenance can be complex and measurable.

The chapter does not infer a universal active-site count, universal MWD/branching outcome or piping property.

### S015-010 — Busico et al. 1999

**PASS.**

Retained use is limited to the defined MgCl2-supported polypropylene catalyst system and the reported statistical-site interpretation of high-resolution stereosequence data.

The chapter explicitly rejects a universal `three sites` conclusion.

### S015-011 — Guo et al. 2025

**PASS.**

Retained use is limited to the defined MgCl2-supported ethylene/alpha-olefin system in which catalyst-state/species evidence was correlated with measured short-chain-branch distribution.

No universal Ti-site/branch-distribution rule or piping-performance transfer is made.

### S015-012 — Kenyon et al. 2022

**PASS.**

Retained use is limited to the defined supported `(EBI)ZrCl2` ethylene-polymerization system. The study is used as a falsification case against the universal shortcut `metallocene = guaranteed narrow monomodal MWD` and as evidence that support/process environment can matter.

It is not used to claim that all supported metallocenes are bimodal or broad-MWD systems.

### S015-013 — De Rosa et al. 2004

**PASS.**

Retained use is limited to the studied zirconocene/propylene catalyst series and the observation that stereoregularity varied within that defined series.

No direct transfer to polyethylene or piping performance is made.

### S015-014 — Czaja & Białek 2001

**PASS.**

Retained use is limited to the three reported supported ethylene/alpha-olefin catalyst systems in which hydrogen addition reduced polymer molecular weight and catalyst activity.

The chapter explicitly rejects a universal hydrogen response across catalyst families.

### S015-015 — Zhang et al. 2021

**PASS.**

Retained use is limited to the defined supported Ziegler–Natta ethylene/propylene copolymerization system and the reported feed-ratio / active-center / fraction behavior.

No universal feed-ratio → branching/composition/property law is asserted.

### S015-016 — Wang et al. 1998

**PASS.**

Retained use is limited to the defined constrained-geometry-catalyst continuous solution ethylene CSTR system and its measured/modelled temperature, mean-residence-time, feed-concentration and long-chain-branching/kinetic history.

No universal temperature or residence-time rule is transferred to unrelated PE systems.

## 4. Claim-boundary audit

PASS.

The final manuscript maintains these distinctions:

`terminology / mechanism authority`

→ `system-specific primary evidence`

→ `measured resin/compound state`

→ `qualified product`

→ `application/system decision`.

The following invalid transfers are explicitly blocked rather than endorsed:

- catalyst family → fixed MWD/branching/comonomer distribution;
- process variable → universal architecture direction;
- measured architecture → automatic SCG/toughness/permeability/fusion conclusion;
- polymerization mechanism → pressure rating/service life/design stress;
- one research resin/system → commercial piping product acceptance.

## 5. Standards versus scientific evidence separation

PASS.

No pressure-piping design standard is represented as governing polymerization reaction mechanisms.

IUPAC sources control scientific terminology/classification; ISO 472 / ISO 1043-1 supply vocabulary/abbreviation context only; primary papers support bounded named-system claims; downstream product/application standards remain the authority for actual piping qualification/design.
