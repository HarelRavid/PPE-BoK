## 1. Validation question

Does the controlled source register support the scientific terminology, nomenclature and bounded material-specific evidence currently used by Working Chapter 014, without treating vocabulary/nomenclature sources as design standards or transferring primary-study results into unsupported piping design values?

## 2. Source hierarchy applied

For Chapter 014, evidence authority is intentionally different from a pressure-design chapter.

The validated hierarchy is:

1. current authoritative ISO public records for plastics vocabulary / abbreviation document identity and lifecycle;
2. current official IUPAC terminology and nomenclature resources for chemistry/polymer terminology;
3. formal IUPAC Recommendations / Technical Reports for specialized scientific terminology such as hydrogen bonding and chemical-structure representation;
4. directly reviewed primary literature for named-material structure→property teaching cases;
5. internal PPE-BoK architecture only for chapter ownership / evidence-transfer logic.

Vocabulary and nomenclature sources control language. They do **not** create allowable stress, pressure, temperature, lifetime, chemical-compatibility limits or product/application qualification requirements.

### 5.2 S014-010 — Sambale et al. (2021)

**Validation result: PASS.**

Primary publication metadata confirms:

### 5.3 S014-011 — Graunke et al. (2016)

**Validation result: PASS WITH TRANSFERABILITY LIMIT.**

Primary publication metadata confirms:

Thorsten Graunke; Katrin Schmitt; Stefan Raible; Jürgen Wöllenstein. *Towards Enhanced Gas Sensor Performance with Fluoropolymer Membranes*. Sensors 16(10), 1605 (2016). DOI `10.3390/s16101605`.

The Chapter 014 use remains methodological: selected membrane results show why simple chemical descriptors are insufficient as a universal transport ranking once polymer-specific structure/morphology/test-form effects enter.

**Prohibited transfer remains:** no piping-grade fluoropolymer permeability ranking or pipe-wall allowance may be derived from this membrane study.

### 5.4 S014-012 — Monson, Moon & Extrand (2009)

**Validation result: PASS WITH TRANSFERABILITY LIMIT.**

Publisher metadata confirms:

L. Monson; Sung In Moon; C. W. Extrand. *Gas permeation resistance of various grades of perfluoroalkoxy–polytetrafluoroethylene copolymers*. Journal of Applied Polymer Science 111(1), 141–147 (2009). DOI `10.1002/app.28858`.

The publisher abstract confirms that specimens were prepared with different cooling techniques; permeability, diffusion and solubility coefficients were measured for H2, N2 and O2; and cooling/process history materially affected measured permeation resistance in the studied specimens.

Chapter 014 correctly uses this as evidence that processing/morphology may materially alter a measured property that cannot be inferred from family/grade chemistry alone.

**Prohibited transfer remains:** no universal PFA pipe permeability value, cooling prescription, service-life claim or product acceptance criterion.

## 6. Evidence-transfer audit

**PASS.**

No current Chapter 014 evidence route authorizes a direct jump from:

- bond type → pipe strength;
- electronegativity / polarity → chemical compatibility;
- hybridization → service temperature;
- repeat unit → material grade;
- primary research specimen → piping design value;
- vocabulary/nomenclature standard → product/application qualification.

The accepted Chapter 014 logic remains:

`chemical / structural observation → mechanism hypothesis → measurable property → direct evidence → confounder review → transferability → material/product/application qualification → engineering decision`

This logic is an internal PPE-BoK engineering reasoning framework, not a normative ISO hierarchy.
