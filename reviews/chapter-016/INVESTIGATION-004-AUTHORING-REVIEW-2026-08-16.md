# Chapter 016 — Investigation 004 Authoring Review

**Date:** 2026-08-16  
**Investigation:** 4 — What Does SEC/GPC Actually Measure, and What Are Its Limits?  
**Disposition:** **PASS**

## 1. Review scope

Reviewed the controlled Investigation 4 authoring candidate against CDB-016, the Technical Outline, active Standards/Evidence Plan and current official ISO 16014 publication pages.

## 2. ISO 16014 method-scope gate

**PASS.**

Official ISO status/source check on 2026-08-16 confirms:

- ISO 16014-1:2019 — Published, Edition 3, stage 90.93 Confirmed;
- ISO 16014-2:2019 — Published/confirmed; universal-calibration route explicitly classified by ISO as a relative method;
- ISO 16014-4:2019 — current high-temperature route, stage 90.93 in ISO/TC 61/SC 5 catalogue;
- ISO 16014-5:2019 — Published, Edition 2, stage 90.93 Confirmed; SEC-LS classified by ISO as an absolute molecular-weight method within its stated scope.

The candidate does not overstate these classifications.

## 3. Separation / detection / calibration logic

**PASS.**

The manuscript correctly separates:

1. chromatographic size-exclusion behaviour;
2. detector response/concentration measurement;
3. calibration or light-scattering model used to assign molar mass.

It does not describe a conventional SEC column as directly weighing molecules.

## 4. Relative versus absolute boundary

**PASS.**

The candidate correctly blocks two common over-inferences:

- `universal calibration = absolute`; and
- `SEC-LS absolute = assumption-free`.

The SEC-LS discussion preserves detector, concentration, sample-quality and applicability limits, including the explicit ISO 16014-5 restrictions for some compositionally varying copolymer systems.

## 5. High-temperature SEC boundary

**PASS.**

High-temperature SEC is presented as a relevant route for polymers requiring elevated-temperature dissolution, not as proof that any particular PE/PP measurement is valid merely because it was run hot.

Detailed solvent, dissolution-time, column and instrument procedure remains deferred to Part V.

## 6. Architecture/calibration boundary

**PASS.**

The candidate correctly explains that branching/composition can alter hydrodynamic behaviour and therefore affect calibration-based apparent molar-mass assignment. It does not infer branch topology from SEC alone.

## 7. Asset review

**PASS.**

`TAB-016-002` has a controlled authoring owner and correctly distinguishes measurement-route directness without ranking laboratory quality or commercial value.

## 8. Chapter-boundary review

**PASS.**

No laboratory SOP, rheological model, morphology treatment or product-design acceptance rule is pulled forward.

## 9. Decision

**PASS. Investigation 5 authorized.**

Investigation 5 shall define branch point, branch count, branch length, placement and topology without reducing branching to one scalar quantity. Specific short-chain versus long-chain branching measurement/property cases remain gated to Investigation 6 and the primary-evidence framework.
