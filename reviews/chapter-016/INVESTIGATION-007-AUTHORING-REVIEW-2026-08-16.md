# Chapter 016 — Investigation 007 Authoring Review

**Date:** 2026-08-16  
**Investigation:** 7 — What Are Crosslinks and Polymer Networks?  
**Disposition:** **PASS**

## 1. Review scope

Reviewed the controlled Investigation 7 authoring candidate against CDB-016, the Technical Outline, active Standards/Evidence Plan, current IUPAC crosslink/network terminology and the official ISO 10147:2011 publication page.

## 2. Crosslink / branch-point terminology gate

**PASS.**

The candidate correctly preserves the IUPAC distinction between a branch point and crosslinking. It specifically retains the useful IUPAC boundary that attachment creating a branch point is not automatically a crosslinking reaction.

## 3. Network terminology gate

**PASS.**

The candidate correctly distinguishes:

- crosslink;
- covalent crosslink;
- network / covalent network;
- physical network;
- entanglement as a separate topological/physical concept to be developed in Investigation 8.

It does not assume all uses of `crosslink` are necessarily covalent and explicitly qualifies permanence when engineering meaning depends on it.

## 4. ISO 10147:2011 gate

**PASS.**

Official ISO status checked 2026-08-16 confirms:

- ISO 10147:2011, Edition 3;
- Published, stage 90.93 Confirmed;
- scope: assessment/estimation of degree of crosslinking in PE-X pipes and fittings by determination of gel content through solvent extraction.

The candidate stays within that scope and does not invent acceptance thresholds.

## 5. Gel-content boundary

**PASS.**

The manuscript explicitly prevents:

- gel content → unique crosslink density;
- gel content → complete network topology;
- higher gel content → universal product quality ranking;
- gel content → joining/pressure/lifetime qualification.

It correctly identifies network defects/heterogeneity as information not uniquely reconstructed by one gel-fraction scalar.

## 6. SEC soluble-fraction boundary

**PASS.**

The candidate correctly explains that SEC on a soluble fraction cannot automatically represent the complete architecture of a crosslinked specimen containing an insoluble network fraction.

## 7. Joining boundary

**PASS.**

The mechanism-level statement that covalent networking can constrain melt mobility is not converted into a joining rule. Fusion performance remains behind direct evidence and Part VII qualification.

## 8. Chapter-boundary review

**PASS.**

No rubber-elasticity/network-theory derivation, detailed extraction SOP, joining qualification rule or product acceptance limit is pulled into Chapter 016.

## 9. Decision

**PASS. Investigation 8 authorized.**

Investigation 8 shall explain entanglement as a topological/physical constraint distinct from permanent covalent crosslinks, while preserving Chapter 019 ownership of detailed rheology, reptation, relaxation and time-dependent constitutive treatment.
