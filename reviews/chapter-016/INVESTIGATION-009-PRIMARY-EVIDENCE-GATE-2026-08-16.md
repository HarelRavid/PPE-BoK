# Chapter 016 — Investigation 009 Primary-Evidence Gate

**Date:** 2026-08-16  
**Investigation:** 9 — How Can Architecture Influence Engineering Behaviour Without Becoming a Design Rule?  
**Disposition:** **PASS FOR S016-013 THROUGH S016-016 ONLY**

## 1. Gate requirement

A named architecture→behaviour case may enter Chapter 016 only when:

1. the material/system is identified;
2. the architecture/material variable is measured or independently characterized rather than inferred only from a label;
3. the downstream output is measured directly;
4. major co-varying variables/confounders are stated;
5. the source supports a bounded conclusion;
6. unsupported conclusions are explicit;
7. transferability is stated.

No candidate is admitted merely because the trend is familiar in polymer engineering.

## 2. Admitted Case A — S016-013: commercial PE pipe materials / SCG-related test performance

**System:** Ten commercial PE100 / PE100RC-type materials, including 1-butene- and 1-hexene-based systems.

**Architecture/material characterization:** high-temperature SEC molecular weight/MWD; branching/comonomer-related descriptors; viscosity/rheological variables.

**Downstream measurement:** SH, CRB and NPT-related slow-crack-growth performance.

**Gate decision:** PASS.

**Supported:** Within the tested set, measured molecular/branching/rheological parameters were experimentally correlated with SCG-related test responses. The case demonstrates that architecture becomes engineering evidence only when paired with actual downstream tests.

**Confounders:** Commercial grades differ in more than one variable; MWD, molecular weight, comonomer/SCB, viscosity and resulting morphology can co-vary.

**Blocked inference:** No single-variable universal law such as `higher M_w = better SCG` or `broader MWD = better pipe` is authorized.

**Transferability:** High relevance to PE pressure-pipe materials; bounded to the tested material set/methods and not a classification/design rule.

## 3. Admitted Case B — S016-014: PE long-chain branching / linear viscoelastic response

**System:** Linear PE / ethylene–α-olefin and metallocene PE systems with independently characterized molecular-weight/branching differences.

**Architecture characterization:** MWD / `M_w`, low-level LCB quantified using solution-property methods and ^13C NMR applicability; SCB/comonomer context.

**Downstream measurement:** zero-shear viscosity and relaxation spectrum / linear viscoelastic behaviour.

**Gate decision:** PASS.

**Supported:** In the studied PE systems, LCB changed linear rheological response relative to linear material at comparable molecular-weight conditions, including increased zero-shear viscosity and a long-time relaxation contribution.

**Confounders:** MWD, SCB/comonomer content and LCB topology/level must remain controlled or reported; rheological inverse interpretation is not unique.

**Blocked inference:** Rheology does not uniquely map complete branch topology; LCB is not a pipe-processing or quality acceptance ranking.

**Transferability:** Strong architecture→rheology teaching case for PE; not a universal magnitude or constitutive relation.

## 4. Admitted Case C — S016-015: UHMWPE molecular weight / interface healing and reentanglement

**System:** Nascent UHMWPE powder molecular-weight series sintered above melting under controlled pressure/time/temperature conditions.

**Architecture variable:** Very-high molecular weight / chain population state across approximately 0.6–10.5 million g mol^-1 `M_v`; interface entanglement recovery investigated experimentally.

**Downstream measurement:** particle-interface consolidation/welding and tensile deformation/interfacial heterogeneity above melting.

**Gate decision:** PASS.

**Supported:** The study demonstrates a molecular-weight dependence of sintering/interface-healing behaviour while also showing that high molecular weight does not simply homogenize the interface entanglement network; healing remains dependent on diffusion, time and temperature.

**Confounders:** UHMWPE powder morphology, sintering route, thermal history, crystallization under tension and interface geometry.

**Blocked inference:** The case does not establish a butt-fusion/electrofusion rule for PE100 or a simple `higher/lower M_w = better weld` ranking.

**Transferability:** Mechanistic interface/interdiffusion case only; direct piping joining transfer remains blocked.

## 5. Admitted Case D — S016-016: PE short-chain branching / morphology and free-volume response under hydrogen

**System:** Four MDPE/HDPE materials under hydrogen/xenon pressurization.

**Architecture characterization:** SCB content/character by liquid-state NMR; phase distribution, chain mobility and free volume by complementary solid-state / ^129Xe NMR methods.

**Downstream/material-state measurement:** crystallinity/phase distribution, mobility, free-volume response under hydrogen, with supporting failure-strain observations.

**Gate decision:** PASS.

**Supported:** Within the tested four-material system, SCB content/branch character co-varied with measured morphology, mobility and free-volume response under hydrogen, supporting an architecture→morphology/free-volume mechanism hypothesis.

**Confounders:** Material grade, density/morphology, branch type/content, phase distribution and specific hydrogen/measurement conditions.

**Blocked inference:** No universal SCB→permeability coefficient, hydrogen-service ranking, RGD rule, pressure rating or lifetime conclusion is authorized.

**Transferability:** Mechanistically relevant to PE/hydrogen, but bounded to the tested materials and structural measurements.

## 6. Cases deliberately not admitted

The following chapter-level statements remain prohibited without a separate direct source gate:

- `higher molecular weight = better pipe`;
- `broader MWD = better processing`;
- `more short-chain branching = better SCG`;
- `more long-chain branching = better processing`;
- `more crosslinking = stronger PE-X`;
- `higher gel content = better product`;
- `more entanglements = better fusion`;
- `more SCB = higher hydrogen permeability`.

These may be hypotheses in a defined system, not general rules.

## 7. Evidence balance / chapter ownership

The four admitted cases intentionally cover different downstream domains:

- S016-013 — SCG-related pipe-material tests;
- S016-014 — rheology;
- S016-015 — interface healing/interdiffusion;
- S016-016 — hydrogen/morphology/free-volume response.

Chapter 016 uses them only to teach evidence routing. Detailed SCG, rheology, morphology, gas transport and joining mechanisms remain owned by downstream chapters.

## 8. Decision

**PASS FOR S016-013 THROUGH S016-016 ONLY.**

Investigation 9 may be authored using these four bounded cases. Any additional named case requires a new direct-primary-evidence review before inclusion.
