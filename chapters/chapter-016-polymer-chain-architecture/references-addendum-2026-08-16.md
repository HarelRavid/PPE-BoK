### S016-013 — Deveci and Fang, 2017 — PE pipe molecular parameters versus SCG test performance

**S. Deveci; D. Fang.**  
**Correlation of molecular parameters, strain hardening modulus and cyclic fatigue test performances of polyethylene materials for pressure pipe applications.**  
*Polymer Testing* 62 (2017), 246–253.  
DOI: `10.1016/j.polymertesting.2017.07.007`.

**Evidence class:** C — directly reviewed primary research.

**System:** Ten commercial PE100 / PE100RC-type polyethylene materials including 1-butene- and 1-hexene-based systems.

**Architecture/material variables characterized:**

- weight-/mass-average molecular weight by high-temperature SEC;
- molecular-weight distribution;
- short-chain branching/comonomer-related structure;
- viscosity/rheological descriptors.

**Downstream outputs measured:**

- strain-hardening (SH) response;
- crack round bar (CRB) cyclic test performance;
- comparison/correlation with notched pipe test (NPT) performance.

**Supported use in Chapter 016:**

Within the ten-material dataset, molecular parameters and SCG-related test responses were experimentally compared and correlations were reported. The study supports teaching that multiple architecture/material variables co-vary with SCG-test performance and that architecture descriptors can become testable SCG hypotheses.

**Do not infer:**

- a universal monotonic `higher M_w = better SCG` rule for all PE;
- independent causality of one variable when MWD, branching, viscosity/comonomer and morphology can co-vary;
- direct conversion from architecture data to PE100/PE100-RC classification or service lifetime;
- applicability to non-PE materials or untested service environments.

**Transferability:** Pressure-pipe PE relevance is high, but the correlations remain bounded to the tested commercial material set and methods.

### S016-014 — Wood-Adams et al., 2000 — measured PE molecular structure versus linear viscoelastic response

**P. M. Wood-Adams; J. M. Dealy; A. W. deGroot; O. D. Redwine.**  
**Effect of Molecular Structure on the Linear Viscoelastic Behavior of Polyethylene.**  
*Macromolecules* 33 (2000), 7489–7499.  
DOI: `10.1021/ma991533z`.

**Evidence class:** C — directly reviewed primary research.

**System:** Linear polyethylene / ethylene–α-olefin copolymers and metallocene polyethylene with controlled differences in molar mass, SCB and low levels of LCB.

**Architecture variables characterized:**

- `M_w` / MWD;
- LCB quantified using solution-property methods, with ^13C NMR applicability demonstrated for low LCB levels;
- SCB/comonomer content for the studied systems.

**Downstream outputs measured:**

- linear viscoelastic response;
- zero-shear viscosity;
- relaxation spectrum / long-time relaxation mode.

**Supported use in Chapter 016:**

For the studied PE systems, LCB changed rheological response relative to linear material at comparable molecular-weight conditions, including increased zero-shear viscosity and an added long-time relaxation contribution. The study supports the proposition that rheology can be highly sensitive to long-chain architecture when MWD/branching are independently characterized.

**Do not infer:**

- rheology uniquely reconstructs all branch topology;
- every LCB PE has the same directional magnitude;
- SCB and LCB are interchangeable;
- rheological sensitivity is a pipe-performance or processing-acceptance criterion.

**Transferability:** Strong for illustrating architecture-sensitive PE rheology; not a universal constitutive law for all polyethylene or other polymers.

### S016-015 — Deplancke et al., 2015 — UHMWPE molecular weight versus particle-interface reentanglement/healing

**T. Deplancke; O. Lame; F. Rousset; R. Seguela; G. Vigier.**  
**Mechanisms of Chain Reentanglement during the Sintering of UHMWPE Nascent Powder: Effect of Molecular Weight.**  
*Macromolecules* 48 (2015), 5328–5338.  
DOI: `10.1021/acs.macromol.5b00618`.

**Evidence class:** C — directly reviewed primary research.

**System:** Nascent UHMWPE powders spanning approximately `0.6 < M_v < 10.5 million g mol^-1`, consolidated/sintered under controlled pressure, temperature and time conditions above melting.

**Architecture variable:** Molecular-weight series / very-long-chain UHMWPE state; entanglement-network recovery investigated through interface-healing experiments.

**Downstream/interface outputs measured:**

- particle-interface consolidation/welding;
- tensile drawing above melting;
- grain/interface deformation heterogeneity;
- chain interdiffusion/reentanglement interpretation as a function of molecular weight and thermal history.

**Supported use in Chapter 016:**

The study demonstrates experimentally that molecular weight and interface-healing kinetics interact in UHMWPE and, crucially, that a higher molecular weight does not simply guarantee rapid homogenization of the entanglement network. Healing of very long-chain interfaces remained time/temperature dependent and heterogeneous under the studied conditions.

**Do not infer:**

- direct applicability of UHMWPE powder sintering kinetics to PE100 butt fusion/electrofusion;
- `higher M_w = better weld` or `lower M_w = better weld`;
- a welding acceptance time/temperature for piping;
- equivalence between particle sintering and qualified pipe fusion.

**Transferability:** Mechanistically valuable for the molar-mass/interdiffusion/entanglement boundary; direct joining transfer is intentionally blocked.

### S016-016 — Han et al., 2026 — PE SCB, morphology/free volume and hydrogen pressurization

**K. S. Han; S. Ko; Y. Shin; E. D. Walter; M. F. N. Taufique; W. Kuang; K. L. Simmons.**  
**Effects of Short-Chain Branching on Morphology and Free Volume in Polyethylene under Hydrogen Pressurization Studied by 1H, 13C, and 129Xe NMR.**  
*The Journal of Physical Chemistry B* 130 (2026), 630–639.  
Published online 2025-12-18.  
DOI: `10.1021/acs.jpcb.5c07592`.

**Evidence class:** C — directly reviewed primary research.

**System:** Four MDPE/HDPE samples investigated under hydrogen/xenon pressurization.

**Architecture/material variables characterized:**

- SCB content and branch character by liquid-state ^1H/^13C NMR;
- semicrystalline phase distribution / chain mobility by solid-state NMR;
- free-volume state using ^129Xe NMR;
- studied SCB levels approximately 1.94–2.66 branches per 1000 carbon atoms.

**Downstream outputs/state measured:**

- phase/crystallinity changes;
- chain mobility;
- free-volume formation;
- hydrogen-associated structural response;
- supporting tensile failure-strain observations.

**Supported use in Chapter 016:**

Within the four studied polyethylene systems, differences in SCB content/branch type were associated with measured differences in crystallinity, chain mobility and free-volume response under hydrogen pressurization. The study supports the bounded hypothesis that architecture can influence morphology/free-volume pathways relevant to gas transport and mechanical response.

**Do not infer:**

- a universal `more SCB = more hydrogen permeability` law;
- direct pipe-wall permeability coefficients from the NMR structural observations;
- universal ranking of hexene/heptene/octene PE pipe grades;
- pressure-rating, RGD, lifetime or hydrogen-service qualification from SCB data alone.

**Transferability:** Highly relevant mechanistically to PE/hydrogen service, but bounded to the four studied materials, pressure/measurement conditions and measured structural outputs.
