## 11. Engineering-Development evidence additions — 2026-08-15

### S015-008 — Basic classification and definitions of polymerization reactions — IUPAC Recommendations 2025, published 2026

- **Organization:** IUPAC.
- **Authors:** Wesley S. Farrell; Daniel J. Keddie; Christine K. Luscombe; John B. Matson; Jan Merna; Graeme Moad; Gregory T. Russell; Lydia Sosa Vargas; Patrick Théato; Paul D. Topham.
- **Publication:** *Pure and Applied Chemistry* 98(7) (2026), 1105–1117.
- **DOI:** `10.1515/pac-2025-0490`.
- **First publication:** 2026-05-12; listed in the July 2026 PAC issue.
- **Evidence class:** A/B — current authoritative IUPAC Recommendation.
- **Role:** controlling current basic polymerization-classification source.

**Controlled hierarchy:**

- `step polymerization` — growth by reactions between monomer, oligomer or polymer molecules of any length;
  - `additive step polymerization` — synonym `polyaddition`;
  - `condensative step polymerization` — synonym `polycondensation`;
- `chain polymerization` — polymerization proceeding through a chain reaction with monomer addition at active sites on growing chains;
  - `additive chain polymerization`;
  - `condensative chain polymerization`.

**Chapter implication:** S015-008 supersedes the initial planning assumption that the step-growth concept should remain merely explanatory. Canonical Chapter 015 uses the published step/chain hierarchy. The older pair `addition polymerization / condensation polymerization` is not used as the controlling top-level taxonomy.

**Boundary:** terminology/classification only; no catalyst-performance, molecular-architecture or piping-performance acceptance follows from the classification.

### S015-009 — Piovano et al. (2018) — MgCl2-based Ziegler–Natta catalyst genesis

- **Citation:** Alessandro Piovano et al., *Genesis of MgCl2-based Ziegler–Natta Catalysts as Probed with Operando Spectroscopy*, *ChemPhysChem* 19(20) (2018), 2662–2671.
- **DOI:** `10.1002/cphc.201800592`.
- **Evidence class:** C — directly reviewed primary research.
- **Studied system:** MgCl2-based Ziegler–Natta catalyst preparation/activation including high-surface-area MgCl2 formation, TiCl4 grafting and triethylaluminum activation; ethylene polymerization test.
- **Supported use:** the investigated catalyst was a complex multi-component system and its active-site genesis involved sequential/simultaneous preparation and activation steps observable by operando methods. Catalyst-precursor / activation history is therefore a legitimate provenance question.
- **Unsupported transfer:** no universal active-site count; no universal PE MWD/branching rule; no claim about every Ziegler–Natta catalyst; no piping-performance conclusion.

### S015-010 — Busico et al. (1999) — PP stereosequence/site-model evidence in one supported Ziegler–Natta system

- **Citation:** Vincenzo Busico et al., *High-Resolution 13C NMR Configurational Analysis of Polypropylene Made with MgCl2-Supported Ziegler–Natta Catalysts. 1. The “Model” System MgCl2/TiCl4–2,6-Dimethylpyridine/Al(C2H5)3*, *Macromolecules* 32(13) (1999), 4173–4182.
- **DOI:** `10.1021/ma981941n`.
- **Evidence class:** C.
- **Studied system:** specified MgCl2-supported Ziegler–Natta polypropylene catalyst system.
- **Supported use:** high-resolution 13C NMR data in the studied PP fractions were better described by the reported three-site statistical model than by the tested two-site models; polymer microstructure can therefore be evidence bearing on catalyst-site models in a defined system.
- **Unsupported transfer:** not a universal `three active sites` rule; not proof that every heterogeneous catalyst has the same site populations; no direct piping-property inference.

### S015-011 — Guo et al. (2025) — active species / short-chain-branch distribution in one PE/alpha-olefin system

- **Citation:** Tao Guo, Chuanding Dong, Xiaokai Cheng, Jingdai Wang, Yongrong Yang, Wei Li, *Elucidating the correlation between active species and branch distribution of polyethylene in Ziegler–Natta catalysis*, *Journal of Catalysis* 446 (2025), 116092.
- **DOI:** `10.1016/j.jcat.2025.116092`.
- **Evidence class:** C.
- **Studied system:** designed spherical MgCl2-supported catalyst for ethylene/alpha-olefin polymerization; preparation/activation strategy and Ti-species-state characterization.
- **Supported use:** the defined catalyst system showed measured correlations between catalyst-state populations and short-chain-branch distribution across polymer molecular-weight fractions; catalyst-state/site hypotheses can be tested against polymer architecture.
- **Unsupported transfer:** no universal Ziegler–Natta branch-distribution rule; no universal Ti-site mapping; no direct SCG, fusion, hydrostatic or other piping-performance conclusion.

### S015-012 — Kenyon et al. (2022) — supported metallocene environment / PE MWD

- **Citation:** Philip Kenyon, D. W. Justin Leung, Zoë R. Turner, Jean-Charles Buffet, Dermot O’Hare, *Tuning Polyethylene Molecular Weight Distributions Using Catalyst Support Composition*, *Macromolecules* 55(9) (2022), 3408–3414.
- **DOI:** `10.1021/acs.macromol.1c02604`.
- **Evidence class:** C.
- **Studied system:** rac-ethylenebis(1-indenyl)zirconium dichloride, `(EBI)ZrCl2`, immobilized on tunable Ni-containing layered-double-hydroxide-derived supports for ethylene polymerization.
- **Supported use:** in the defined system, one immobilized metallocene precursor/support environment produced bimodal polyethylene under reported conditions and MWD response changed with support composition / reaction conditions. This falsifies the universal shortcut `metallocene = guaranteed narrow monomodal MWD` and shows support/process environment can be part of catalyst provenance.
- **Unsupported transfer:** not every supported metallocene produces bimodal/broad MWD; no universal support effect; no direct commercial pipe-resin or piping-performance conclusion.

### S015-013 — De Rosa et al. (2004) — stereoregularity variation across one zirconocene PP catalyst series

- **Citation:** Claudio De Rosa, Finizia Auriemma, Annunziata Di Capua, Luigi Resconi, Simona Guidotti, Isabella Camurati, Ilya E. Nifant’ev, Ilya P. Laishevtsev, *Structure–Property Correlations in Polypropylene from Metallocene Catalysts: Stereodefective, Regioregular Isotactic Polypropylene*, *Journal of the American Chemical Society* 126(51) (2004), 17040–17049.
- **DOI:** `10.1021/ja045684f`.
- **Evidence class:** C.
- **Studied system:** series of MAO-activated C1-symmetric zirconocene catalysts for propene polymerization.
- **Supported use:** the studied catalyst series produced polypropylene with different degrees of stereoregularity; `metallocene` therefore does not define one universal stereochemical outcome.
- **Unsupported transfer:** no universal metallocene stereoregularity rule; no transfer from the studied PP systems to PE or to piping performance.

### S015-014 — Czaja & Białek (2001) — hydrogen response in three supported ethylene/alpha-olefin copolymerization systems

- **Citation:** Krystyna Czaja and Marzena Białek, *Microstructure of ethylene-1-hexene and ethylene-1-octene copolymers obtained over Ziegler–Natta catalysts supported on MgCl2(THF)2*, *Polymer* 42(6) (2001), 2289–2297.
- **DOI:** `10.1016/S0032-3861(00)00621-2`.
- **Evidence class:** C.
- **Studied systems:** ethylene copolymerization with 1-hexene or 1-octene in hydrogen using three supported catalyst systems: `MgCl2(THF)2/VOCl3/Et2AlCl`, `MgCl2(THF)2/VCl4/Et2AlCl`, `MgCl2(THF)2/TiCl4/Et2AlCl`.
- **Supported use:** in all three studied systems, hydrogen addition reduced polymer molecular weight and catalyst activity; 13C NMR was used to investigate copolymer microstructure. Hydrogen is therefore a legitimate system-specific process/transfer variable.
- **Unsupported transfer:** no universal hydrogen direction/magnitude across all catalysts; no universal mechanism; no direct piping-property conclusion.

### S015-015 — Zhang et al. (2021) — E/P feed-ratio response in one supported Ziegler–Natta system

- **Citation:** Biao Zhang, Qingyun Qian, Pengjia Yang, Baiyu Jiang, Zhisheng Fu, Zhiqiang Fan, *Responses of a Supported Ziegler–Natta Catalyst to Comonomer Feed Ratios in Ethylene–Propylene Copolymerization: Differentiation of Active Centers with Different Catalytic Features*, *Industrial & Engineering Chemistry Research* 60(12) (2021), 4575–4588.
- **DOI:** `10.1021/acs.iecr.1c00104`.
- **Evidence class:** C.
- **Studied system:** E/P copolymerization with a `TiCl4/Di/MgCl2–TEA/De` catalyst at varied E/P feed ratios.
- **Supported use:** the study directly varied E/P feed ratio and measured changes in active-center distribution among fractionated copolymer populations / microstructure in the defined catalyst system. Comonomer-feed strategy can therefore be a legitimate architecture-hypothesis input.
- **Unsupported transfer:** no universal feed-ratio → composition/branching rule; no direct transfer to unrelated PE/alpha-olefin pipe resins; no direct property/qualification conclusion.

### S015-016 — Wang et al. (1998) — temperature / residence / feed history in continuous solution ethylene polymerization

- **Citation:** Wen-Jun Wang, Dajing Yan, Shiping Zhu, Archie E. Hamielec, *Kinetics of Long Chain Branching in Continuous Solution Polymerization of Ethylene Using Constrained Geometry Metallocene*, *Macromolecules* 31(25) (1998), 8677–8683.
- **DOI:** `10.1021/ma980914u`.
- **Evidence class:** C.
- **Studied system:** high-temperature/high-pressure CSTR solution polymerization of ethylene with a constrained-geometry catalyst system in Isopar E; reported pressure 500 psig.
- **Supported use:** polymerization temperature, mean residence time and ethylene feed concentration were controlled variables; the study measured/modelled long-chain-branching / active-center / transfer behavior in that defined continuous process. Time-and-condition history is therefore a legitimate architecture-hypothesis input.
- **Unsupported transfer:** no universal temperature → LCB rule; no universal residence-time effect; no transfer of reported kinetic parameters to other catalysts/reactors; no direct piping-performance conclusion.
