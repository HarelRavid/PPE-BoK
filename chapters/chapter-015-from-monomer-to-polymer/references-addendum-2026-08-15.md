## S015-008 — Basic classification and definitions of polymerization reactions

**Organization:** IUPAC  
**Authors:** Wesley S. Farrell; Daniel J. Keddie; Christine K. Luscombe; John B. Matson; Jan Merna; Graeme Moad; Gregory T. Russell; Lydia Sosa Vargas; Patrick Théato; Paul D. Topham

**Volume / issue / pages:** 98(7), 1105–1117  
**First publication:** 2026-05-12  
**DOI:** `10.1515/pac-2025-0490`  
**IUPAC publication checkpoint:** listed in the July 2026 PAC issue / IUPAC Recommendation page

### Authority / role

Current IUPAC Recommendation updating the basic polymerization-classification framework previously represented by the 1994 Recommendation.

### Controlled classification for Chapter 015

The Recommendation identifies two common top-level mechanism classes:

1. **step polymerization** — growth by reactions between monomer, oligomer or polymer molecules of any length;
   - **additive step polymerization**, synonym **polyaddition**;
   - **condensative step polymerization**, synonym **polycondensation**.
2. **chain polymerization** — polymerization proceeding by a chain reaction with monomer molecules adding to active sites on polymer chains;
   - **additive chain polymerization**;
   - **condensative chain polymerization**.

### Chapter use

- controlling source for `FIG-015-001` and `TAB-015-001`;
- controlling source for the Investigation 2 distinction between mechanism class and whether a low-molar-mass by-product is formed;
- supersedes the Chapter 015 planning assumption that `step-growth` should remain merely explanatory rather than part of a formal IUPAC classification;
- preserves the warning that the historical top-level pair `addition polymerization / condensation polymerization` is scientifically inadequate.

### Evidence boundary

This Recommendation controls terminology/classification only. It does not establish:

- catalyst performance;
- molecular-weight distribution;
- branching or comonomer distribution;
- morphology;
- mechanical/fracture properties;
- piping pressure/lifetime/service acceptance.

Those remain subject to the Chapter 015 evidence ladder and downstream qualification framework.
