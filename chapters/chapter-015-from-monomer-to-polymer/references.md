# Chapter 015 — Standards and Evidence Plan

**Working chapter:** 015 — From Monomer to Polymer: Polymerization, Catalysts and Process–Structure Relationships  
**PDS baseline:** 1.0  
**Status:** Active Engineering-Development Evidence Plan  
**Research checkpoint:** 2026-08-15

## 1. Evidence objective

Chapter 015 is a polymerization-mechanism and process-provenance chapter. Its evidence system must separate three layers:

1. **controlled terminology and classification**;
2. **stable mechanism science**;
3. **named material/catalyst/process claims requiring direct primary evidence**.

No terminology source, catalyst family name or mechanism description may be promoted directly into a piping-product acceptance criterion.

## 2. Evidence classes

| Class | Meaning | Permitted use |
|---|---|---|
| A | Current authoritative standard / official scientific terminology source | Controlled terminology, abbreviations and status |
| B | IUPAC Recommendation / recognized scientific recommendation | Mechanism and classification framework |
| C | Directly reviewed primary research | Named material/catalyst/process outcome claims |
| D | Established academic reference / review | Teaching context and navigation, not sole support for load-bearing named claims |
| E | Manufacturer / secondary / educational source | Navigation or provenance lead only unless independently validated |

## 3. Authoritative terminology and standards path

- **Role:** current controlled terminology source.
- **Authoring rule:** record term identifier/DOI when a definition is load-bearing; recheck online version at final Standards/Evidence Validation because the compendium is continuously maintained.

- heterogeneous catalysis coordination polymerization — `09004`;
- homogeneous catalysis coordination polymerization — `09005`;
- metallocene polymerization — `09006`;
- insertion polymerization — `08911`.

### S015-002 — Glossary of Basic Terms in Polymer Science — IUPAC Recommendations 1996

- **Publication:** Pure and Applied Chemistry 68 (1996), 2287–2311.
- **Role:** classical source for polymerization, chain polymerization, polyaddition and polycondensation terminology retained in current Gold Book entries.
- **Key control:** the earlier term `addition polymerization` embraced both current polyaddition and chain polymerization; therefore it must not be used as the chapter's formal two-box classification.

### S015-003 — Glossary of terms related to kinetics, thermodynamics, and mechanisms of polymerization — IUPAC Recommendations 2008

- **Publication:** Pure and Applied Chemistry 80 (2008), 2163–2193.
- **DOI:** `10.1351/pac200880102163`.
- **Role:** mechanism/kinetics terminology and bridge between earlier terminology and modern chain-polymerization language.

### S015-004 — Terminology for Chain Polymerization — IUPAC Recommendations 2021, published 2022

- **Publication:** Pure and Applied Chemistry 94 (2022), 1093–1147.
- **Role:** current chain-polymerization terminology, including active-site/chain-carrier concepts, coordination polymerization and modern qualification of catalyst terminology.
- **Controlled implications:**
  - chain polymerization is a chain reaction in which propagation involves monomer and reactive site(s) on a growing chain with regeneration of the reactive site;
  - initiation and propagation are fundamental; termination and chain transfer may or may not occur depending on the route;
  - coordination polymerization requires preliminary coordination of monomer with a chain carrier;
  - `metal-catalyzed polymerization` is deprecated as an unexplained synonym for coordination polymerization;
  - heterogeneous coordination polymerization of polyolefins is sometimes known as Ziegler–Natta polymerization, while named reactions are deprecated in the controlled terminology;
  - metallocene polymerization is one form of homogeneous coordination polymerization.

### S015-005 — IUPAC Purple Book / Compendium of Polymer Terminology and Nomenclature

- **Role:** polymer/macromolecular terminology and nomenclature background.
- **Use:** cross-check monomer, polymer, macromolecule and source-based/structure-based language when required.

### S015-006 — ISO 472:2013 + Amendment 1:2018 — Plastics — Vocabulary

- **Status checked:** 2026-08-15.
- **ISO status:** Published, stage `90.92 — International Standard to be revised`.
- **Current development note:** the ISO 472 vocabulary family is being split/revised through new parts; publication-time lifecycle recheck is mandatory.
- **Role in Chapter 015:** plastics vocabulary cross-reference only.
- **Not authority for:** polymerization mechanism, catalyst mechanism, kinetics or piping performance.

### S015-007 — ISO 1043-1:2011 + Amendment 1:2016 — Plastics — Symbols and abbreviated terms — Part 1

- **Status checked:** 2026-08-15.
- **ISO status:** Published / Confirmed, stage `90.93`.
- **Role:** polymer abbreviations and symbol discipline only.
- **Not authority for:** polymerization mechanism or performance.

## 5. Investigation-specific evidence plan

### Investigation 1

Evidence level: A/B plus stable explanatory science.

Required support:
- polymerization/process history is conceptually upstream of chain architecture;
- family name/monomer identity alone does not constitute a complete material state;
- mechanism/provenance information is an evidence-routing input, not a qualification result.

No named catalyst/property example is authorized here.

### Investigation 2

Evidence level: A/B.

Mandatory source check:
- S015-001, S015-002, S015-003, S015-004.

Critical wording controls:
- historical `addition polymerization` warning;
- polyaddition versus chain polymerization distinction;
- condensative chain polymerization as counterexample to simplistic by-product-based classification.

### Investigation 3

Evidence level: A/B.

Mandatory terms:
- chain carrier / active site;
- chain initiation;
- chain propagation;
- chain termination;
- chain transfer.

No statement that termination/transfer is mandatory for every chain polymerization.

### Investigation 4

Evidence level: A/B for general radical mechanism; C for named monomer/process trends.

Primary-evidence gate triggers if manuscript claims a specific effect of temperature, initiator concentration, transfer agent or process condition on a named polymer's molar-mass/architecture output.

### Investigation 5

Evidence level: A/B for polyaddition/polycondensation classification; D/B for idealized step-growth teaching equation if retained.

Any Carothers-type relationship must state assumptions including functionality/stoichiometry/idealization and must not become a commercial-grade prediction.

### Investigation 6

Evidence level: A/B.

Mandatory modern terminology:
- coordination polymerization;
- chain carrier / coordination complex;
- catalyst precursor / activator where applicable;
- insertion/polyinsertion only if definition is controlled.

### Investigation 7

Evidence level: A/B for terminology; C for claims about heterogeneous catalyst site distribution or resulting PE/PP architecture.

Do not author `multi-site catalyst → broad MWD` as a universal rule without direct primary evidence and a bounded system description.

### Investigation 8

Evidence level: A/B for homogeneous/metallocene terminology; C for stereoregularity/comonomer-placement/MWD claims in named systems.

No superiority ranking (`metallocene is better`) is permitted.

### Investigation 9

Evidence level: C required for each retained material/process-specific case.

Potential evidence questions:
- hydrogen/chain transfer in polyolefin polymerization;
- comonomer-feed effects;
- catalyst-site environment versus comonomer incorporation;
- temperature effects on chain transfer / molecular outcome;
- reactor/process history where measured architecture data exist.

Each case requires the controlled matrix defined in CDB-015.

### Investigation 10

Evidence level: synthesis of A/B/C already validated.

No new named technical case should be introduced here unless it independently passes the Investigation 9 gate.

## 6. Primary-literature gate register

No material/process-specific case is pre-approved solely by this plan.

Before Investigation 9 canonical authoring, create a case matrix and mark each candidate:

- `CANDIDATE`;
- `DIRECT SOURCE LOCATED`;
- `DIRECT SOURCE REVIEWED`;
- `SUPPORTED CLAIM BOUNDED`;
- `TRANSFERABILITY DISPOSITIONED`;
- `APPROVED FOR AUTHORING`.

Minimum fields:

| Field | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Monomer/feed | exact identity and composition as reported |
| Catalyst/initiator | exact reported system at useful level |
| Process variable | controlled variable(s) and relevant conditions |
| Measured architecture output | direct measurement, not inferred marketing label |
| Property output | only if actually measured |
| Confounders | catalyst support/site distribution, temperature, feed, conversion, residence history, additives/post-processing as applicable |
| Supported conclusion | bounded to source |
| Unsupported conclusion | explicit |
| Piping transferability | qualitative mechanism / material state / product-level limitation |

## 7. Quantitative evidence policy

No quantitative relation is authorized merely because it is common in polymer textbooks.

Before inclusion, record:

- source;
- derivation/definition status;
- assumptions;
- symbols/units;
- validity range or conceptual limits;
- whether the relation belongs in Chapter 015 or Chapter 016.

Candidate equation status at Definition of Ready:

| Candidate | Status |
|---|---|
| chain propagation/rate notation | HOLD — include only if it improves mechanism understanding |
| ideal Carothers relationship | HOLD — include only with explicit ideal assumptions and ownership boundary |
| chain-transfer relationship | HOLD — requires clear engineering use and evidence |

## 8. Evidence-boundary rules

1. Terminology authority is not design authority.
2. Mechanism plausibility is not measured architecture.
3. Measured resin architecture is not product qualification.
4. Product qualification is not application/system suitability.
5. A catalyst-family label is not a catalyst specification.
6. A reactor/process-family label is not a complete material provenance record.
7. A supplier statement such as `single-site`, `metallocene`, `Ziegler–Natta`, `bimodal` or `advanced catalyst` must be routed to measured material/product evidence before it affects piping acceptance.
8. Every numerical transfer from research specimen/resin to piping compound/product requires an explicit transferability basis.

## 9. Current source-status checkpoint

At the 2026-08-15 planning checkpoint:

- IUPAC chain-polymerization terminology Recommendation is published and is the controlling modern terminology path for the relevant mechanism terms.
- Gold Book 5th ed. online v5.0.0 is the active controlled online terminology source; individual term versions must be rechecked at final validation.
- ISO 472:2013 remains published but is at stage 90.92 and under replacement/revision through a multipart vocabulary programme; it must be rechecked at publication time.
- ISO 1043-1:2011 remains published/confirmed at stage 90.93.

## 10. Definition-of-Ready evidence disposition

**PASS for entering Engineering Development.**

The authoritative terminology path is identified, lifecycle holds are explicit, named material/process claims are gated behind direct primary evidence, and no missing standard is required to begin Investigation 1. Later Investigations that need named process/catalyst claims remain blocked at their own evidence gates until those sources are directly reviewed.

## 11. Engineering-Development evidence additions — 2026-08-15

### S015-008 — Basic classification and definitions of polymerization reactions — IUPAC Recommendations 2025, published 2026

- **Organization:** IUPAC.
- **Authors:** Wesley S. Farrell; Daniel J. Keddie; Christine K. Luscombe; John B. Matson; Jan Merna; Graeme Moad; Gregory T. Russell; Lydia Sosa Vargas; Patrick Théato; Paul D. Topham.
- **Publication:** *Pure and Applied Chemistry* 98(7) (2026), 1105–1117.
- **DOI:** `10.1515/pac-2025-0490`.
- **First publication:** 2026-05-12; listed in the July 2026 PAC issue.
- **Evidence class:** A/B — current authoritative IUPAC Recommendation.
- **Role:** controlling current basic polymerization-classification source.

**Controlled hierarchy:**

- `step polymerization` — growth by reactions between monomer, oligomer or polymer molecules of any length;
  - `additive step polymerization` — synonym `polyaddition`;
  - `condensative step polymerization` — synonym `polycondensation`;
- `chain polymerization` — polymerization proceeding through a chain reaction with monomer addition at active sites on growing chains;
  - `additive chain polymerization`;
  - `condensative chain polymerization`.

**Chapter implication:** S015-008 supersedes the initial planning assumption that the step-growth concept should remain merely explanatory. Canonical Chapter 015 uses the published step/chain hierarchy. The older pair `addition polymerization / condensation polymerization` is not used as the controlling top-level taxonomy.

**Boundary:** terminology/classification only; no catalyst-performance, molecular-architecture or piping-performance acceptance follows from the classification.

### S015-009 — Piovano et al. (2018) — MgCl2-based Ziegler–Natta catalyst genesis

- **Citation:** Alessandro Piovano et al., *Genesis of MgCl2-based Ziegler–Natta Catalysts as Probed with Operando Spectroscopy*, *ChemPhysChem* 19(20) (2018), 2662–2671.
- **DOI:** `10.1002/cphc.201800592`.
- **Evidence class:** C — directly reviewed primary research.
- **Studied system:** MgCl2-based Ziegler–Natta catalyst preparation/activation including high-surface-area MgCl2 formation, TiCl4 grafting and triethylaluminum activation; ethylene polymerization test.
- **Supported use:** the investigated catalyst was a complex multi-component system and its active-site genesis involved sequential/simultaneous preparation and activation steps observable by operando methods. Catalyst-precursor / activation history is therefore a legitimate provenance question.
- **Unsupported transfer:** no universal active-site count; no universal PE MWD/branching rule; no claim about every Ziegler–Natta catalyst; no piping-performance conclusion.

### S015-010 — Busico et al. (1999) — PP stereosequence/site-model evidence in one supported Ziegler–Natta system

- **Citation:** Vincenzo Busico et al., *High-Resolution 13C NMR Configurational Analysis of Polypropylene Made with MgCl2-Supported Ziegler–Natta Catalysts. 1. The “Model” System MgCl2/TiCl4–2,6-Dimethylpyridine/Al(C2H5)3*, *Macromolecules* 32(13) (1999), 4173–4182.
- **DOI:** `10.1021/ma981941n`.
- **Evidence class:** C.
- **Studied system:** specified MgCl2-supported Ziegler–Natta polypropylene catalyst system.
- **Supported use:** high-resolution 13C NMR data in the studied PP fractions were better described by the reported three-site statistical model than by the tested two-site models; polymer microstructure can therefore be evidence bearing on catalyst-site models in a defined system.
- **Unsupported transfer:** not a universal `three active sites` rule; not proof that every heterogeneous catalyst has the same site populations; no direct piping-property inference.

### S015-011 — Guo et al. (2025) — active species / short-chain-branch distribution in one PE/alpha-olefin system

- **Citation:** Tao Guo, Chuanding Dong, Xiaokai Cheng, Jingdai Wang, Yongrong Yang, Wei Li, *Elucidating the correlation between active species and branch distribution of polyethylene in Ziegler–Natta catalysis*, *Journal of Catalysis* 446 (2025), 116092.
- **DOI:** `10.1016/j.jcat.2025.116092`.
- **Evidence class:** C.
- **Studied system:** designed spherical MgCl2-supported catalyst for ethylene/alpha-olefin polymerization; preparation/activation strategy and Ti-species-state characterization.
- **Supported use:** the defined catalyst system showed measured correlations between catalyst-state populations and short-chain-branch distribution across polymer molecular-weight fractions; catalyst-state/site hypotheses can be tested against polymer architecture.
- **Unsupported transfer:** no universal Ziegler–Natta branch-distribution rule; no universal Ti-site mapping; no direct SCG, fusion, hydrostatic or other piping-performance conclusion.

### S015-012 — Kenyon et al. (2022) — supported metallocene environment / PE MWD

- **Citation:** Philip Kenyon, D. W. Justin Leung, Zoë R. Turner, Jean-Charles Buffet, Dermot O’Hare, *Tuning Polyethylene Molecular Weight Distributions Using Catalyst Support Composition*, *Macromolecules* 55(9) (2022), 3408–3414.
- **DOI:** `10.1021/acs.macromol.1c02604`.
- **Evidence class:** C.
- **Studied system:** rac-ethylenebis(1-indenyl)zirconium dichloride, `(EBI)ZrCl2`, immobilized on tunable Ni-containing layered-double-hydroxide-derived supports for ethylene polymerization.
- **Supported use:** in the defined system, one immobilized metallocene precursor/support environment produced bimodal polyethylene under reported conditions and MWD response changed with support composition / reaction conditions. This falsifies the universal shortcut `metallocene = guaranteed narrow monomodal MWD` and shows support/process environment can be part of catalyst provenance.
- **Unsupported transfer:** not every supported metallocene produces bimodal/broad MWD; no universal support effect; no direct commercial pipe-resin or piping-performance conclusion.

### S015-013 — De Rosa et al. (2004) — stereoregularity variation across one zirconocene PP catalyst series

- **Citation:** Claudio De Rosa, Finizia Auriemma, Annunziata Di Capua, Luigi Resconi, Simona Guidotti, Isabella Camurati, Ilya E. Nifant’ev, Ilya P. Laishevtsev, *Structure–Property Correlations in Polypropylene from Metallocene Catalysts: Stereodefective, Regioregular Isotactic Polypropylene*, *Journal of the American Chemical Society* 126(51) (2004), 17040–17049.
- **DOI:** `10.1021/ja045684f`.
- **Evidence class:** C.
- **Studied system:** series of MAO-activated C1-symmetric zirconocene catalysts for propene polymerization.
- **Supported use:** the studied catalyst series produced polypropylene with different degrees of stereoregularity; `metallocene` therefore does not define one universal stereochemical outcome.
- **Unsupported transfer:** no universal metallocene stereoregularity rule; no transfer from the studied PP systems to PE or to piping performance.

### S015-014 — Czaja & Białek (2001) — hydrogen response in three supported ethylene/alpha-olefin copolymerization systems

- **Citation:** Krystyna Czaja and Marzena Białek, *Microstructure of ethylene-1-hexene and ethylene-1-octene copolymers obtained over Ziegler–Natta catalysts supported on MgCl2(THF)2*, *Polymer* 42(6) (2001), 2289–2297.
- **DOI:** `10.1016/S0032-3861(00)00621-2`.
- **Evidence class:** C.
- **Studied systems:** ethylene copolymerization with 1-hexene or 1-octene in hydrogen using three supported catalyst systems: `MgCl2(THF)2/VOCl3/Et2AlCl`, `MgCl2(THF)2/VCl4/Et2AlCl`, `MgCl2(THF)2/TiCl4/Et2AlCl`.
- **Supported use:** in all three studied systems, hydrogen addition reduced polymer molecular weight and catalyst activity; 13C NMR was used to investigate copolymer microstructure. Hydrogen is therefore a legitimate system-specific process/transfer variable.
- **Unsupported transfer:** no universal hydrogen direction/magnitude across all catalysts; no universal mechanism; no direct piping-property conclusion.

### S015-015 — Zhang et al. (2021) — E/P feed-ratio response in one supported Ziegler–Natta system

- **Citation:** Biao Zhang, Qingyun Qian, Pengjia Yang, Baiyu Jiang, Zhisheng Fu, Zhiqiang Fan, *Responses of a Supported Ziegler–Natta Catalyst to Comonomer Feed Ratios in Ethylene–Propylene Copolymerization: Differentiation of Active Centers with Different Catalytic Features*, *Industrial & Engineering Chemistry Research* 60(12) (2021), 4575–4588.
- **DOI:** `10.1021/acs.iecr.1c00104`.
- **Evidence class:** C.
- **Studied system:** E/P copolymerization with a `TiCl4/Di/MgCl2–TEA/De` catalyst at varied E/P feed ratios.
- **Supported use:** the study directly varied E/P feed ratio and measured changes in active-center distribution among fractionated copolymer populations / microstructure in the defined catalyst system. Comonomer-feed strategy can therefore be a legitimate architecture-hypothesis input.
- **Unsupported transfer:** no universal feed-ratio → composition/branching rule; no direct transfer to unrelated PE/alpha-olefin pipe resins; no direct property/qualification conclusion.

### S015-016 — Wang et al. (1998) — temperature / residence / feed history in continuous solution ethylene polymerization

- **Citation:** Wen-Jun Wang, Dajing Yan, Shiping Zhu, Archie E. Hamielec, *Kinetics of Long Chain Branching in Continuous Solution Polymerization of Ethylene Using Constrained Geometry Metallocene*, *Macromolecules* 31(25) (1998), 8677–8683.
- **DOI:** `10.1021/ma980914u`.
- **Evidence class:** C.
- **Studied system:** high-temperature/high-pressure CSTR solution polymerization of ethylene with a constrained-geometry catalyst system in Isopar E; reported pressure 500 psig.
- **Supported use:** polymerization temperature, mean residence time and ethylene feed concentration were controlled variables; the study measured/modelled long-chain-branching / active-center / transfer behavior in that defined continuous process. Time-and-condition history is therefore a legitimate architecture-hypothesis input.
- **Unsupported transfer:** no universal temperature → LCB rule; no universal residence-time effect; no transfer of reported kinetic parameters to other catalysts/reactors; no direct piping-performance conclusion.

## 13. Final evidence boundary

The chapter evidence hierarchy remains:

`authoritative terminology / mechanism` → `direct named-system primary evidence` → `measured resin/compound state` → `qualified product` → `application/system decision`.

No source in S015-001 through S015-016 is, by itself, a pressure-piping design or product-acceptance authority.
