# Chapter 0 — How to Use This Book

## Why This Chapter Matters

Every industrial piping system eventually reaches one of two outcomes: it performs safely and reliably throughout its intended service life, or it experiences premature failures, unnecessary downtime, costly repairs, or safety incidents.

The difference rarely depends on a single weld, fitting, or calculation. It depends on hundreds of engineering decisions made throughout the life cycle of the system. This book was written to help engineers make those decisions using engineering principles, international standards, and industrial experience.

It is not a welding manual, a material catalogue, or a replacement for applicable codes. It is an engineering reference that explains why decisions are made, how they should be made, and how to recognize when they have been made incorrectly.

## 0.1 Why This Book Exists

Plastic piping systems are essential in water and wastewater, chemical processing, hydrogen, semiconductor manufacturing, mining, food and beverage, pharmaceuticals, energy, and infrastructure. Yet the knowledge required to engineer them is fragmented across standards, textbooks, academic literature, manufacturer documentation, company procedures, and field experience.

PPE-BoK consolidates that knowledge into one coherent engineering discipline.

## 0.2 What This Book Is

This book supports engineers through the complete life cycle of industrial plastic piping systems:

- defining process requirements;
- selecting materials;
- performing hydraulic and mechanical design;
- selecting joining technologies;
- managing construction and installation;
- performing inspection and quality assurance;
- planning testing and commissioning;
- operating, maintaining, modifying, and repairing systems;
- investigating failures and improving future designs.

## 0.3 What This Book Is Not

This book does not replace:

- laws and regulatory requirements;
- international standards and engineering codes;
- manufacturer installation instructions;
- project specifications;
- qualified engineering judgement.

Mandatory project requirements always take precedence. The book provides the background needed to understand, interpret, and apply them responsibly.

## 0.4 Intended Readers

The primary audience includes process, mechanical, project, plant, reliability, commissioning, QA/QC, and asset-integrity engineers. It is also intended for inspectors, supervisors, contractors, consultants, maintenance personnel, and advanced engineering students.

No prior specialization in polymer engineering is assumed.

## 0.5 Engineering Philosophy

Every piping system exists to serve a process. Engineering decisions should therefore begin with the process objective and service conditions—not with a preferred material, supplier, or joining method.

Throughout this book, every important topic is examined through five questions:

1. What is it?
2. Why does it behave this way?
3. Why does it matter to the system?
4. How should an engineer use the knowledge?
5. How can it fail?

## 0.6 Evidence and Uncertainty

The book distinguishes between:

- mandatory requirements;
- established physical principles;
- engineering consensus;
- peer-reviewed findings;
- manufacturer-specific guidance;
- engineering judgement;
- unresolved knowledge gaps.

Where evidence is incomplete or conflicting, the uncertainty is stated explicitly.

## 0.7 Organization of the Book

The book follows the life cycle of an industrial plastic piping system:

1. engineering foundations;
2. process definition and requirements;
3. material selection;
4. hydraulic and process design;
5. mechanical design;
6. joining technologies;
7. construction and installation;
8. inspection and quality assurance;
9. testing and commissioning;
10. operation, maintenance, and modification;
11. failure analysis and root-cause investigation;
12. future developments.

## 0.8 How to Read It

Newcomers should read sequentially. Experienced engineers may use individual chapters as references. Cross-references connect design, construction, operation, and failure mechanisms so that no topic is treated as isolated from the complete system.

## 0.9 The Goal

The goal is simple:

> Help engineers make better engineering decisions throughout the complete life cycle of industrial plastic piping systems.

A chapter succeeds only when it improves a real decision in design, construction, operation, inspection, maintenance, or failure investigation.
