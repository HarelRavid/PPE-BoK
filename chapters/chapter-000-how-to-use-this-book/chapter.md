# Chapter 0 — How to Use This Book

## Why This Chapter Matters

Industrial piping systems succeed or fail over a life cycle, not at a single design moment.

A system may operate reliably for decades, or it may experience shortened life, repeated intervention, loss of availability, premature failure, or unacceptable safety risk. Those outcomes are shaped by the interaction of engineering decisions made during design, procurement, construction, commissioning, operation, maintenance, modification, and failure response.

A single defect, joint, fitting, calculation, or operating event may sometimes initiate a failure. But system performance cannot usually be understood from one component property alone. It depends on how the process, material, geometry, loads, joints, supports, environment, workmanship, operation, inspection, and maintenance interact.

This book was written to help engineers make those decisions using engineering principles, standards, evidence, and disciplined judgement.

It is not a welding manual, a material catalogue, or a substitute for applicable legal, regulatory, code, standard, manufacturer, or project requirements. It is an engineering reference intended to explain **why decisions are made, how they should be made, what evidence supports them, and when a previous decision must be reopened.**

---

## 0.1 Why This Book Exists

Plastic piping systems are used in water and wastewater, chemical processing, hydrogen, semiconductor manufacturing, mining, food and beverage, pharmaceuticals, energy, infrastructure, and many other industrial services.

The knowledge required to engineer these systems is fragmented across:

- standards and codes;
- textbooks;
- academic literature;
- manufacturer documentation;
- project specifications;
- company procedures;
- operating experience;
- inspection and failure evidence;
- engineering judgement.

PPE-BoK brings those sources together into one coherent engineering discipline.

The purpose is not to replace the original sources. It is to help the engineer understand how they connect.

## 0.2 What This Book Is

This book supports engineering decisions throughout the complete life cycle of industrial plastic piping systems, including:

- defining process requirements;
- establishing the Design Basis;
- selecting materials and products;
- performing hydraulic and mechanical design;
- selecting joining technologies;
- managing construction and installation;
- performing inspection and quality assurance;
- planning testing and commissioning;
- operating and monitoring systems;
- maintaining, modifying, and repairing systems;
- assessing remaining life;
- investigating failures;
- feeding lessons learned back into future designs.

The book repeatedly connects technical knowledge to a practical question:

> **What engineering decision does this information support?**

---

## 0.3 What This Book Is Not

This book does not replace:

- laws or regulatory requirements;
- mandatory engineering codes;
- adopted standards;
- project specifications and owner requirements;
- manufacturer requirements within their applicable scope;
- approved engineering procedures;
- qualified engineering judgement.

Where requirements conflict, the engineer must determine the governing hierarchy for the jurisdiction, contract, project, and application.

As a general rule, a project specification or handbook recommendation cannot override a legal, regulatory, or mandatory code requirement.

This book therefore provides **engineering interpretation and navigation**, not authority to bypass governing requirements.

## 0.5 Engineering Philosophy

Every piping system exists to serve a process.

Engineering decisions should therefore begin with the process objective and the Design Basis—not with a preferred material, supplier, product, SDR, joining method, or previous project solution.

Throughout this book, important topics are examined through five recurring questions:

1. **What is it?**
2. **Why does it behave this way?**
3. **Why does it matter to the system?**
4. **How should an engineer use the knowledge?**
5. **How can it fail or be misapplied?**

These five questions form the book's recurring engineering lens.

A calculation, property, test result, or standard requirement becomes useful only when its role in the engineering decision is understood.

## 0.6 Evidence and Uncertainty

The book distinguishes between different kinds of evidence because they do not carry the same authority or engineering meaning.

Typical categories include:

- mandatory legal, regulatory, code, or adopted-standard requirements;
- established physical principles;
- standards-based methods and definitions;
- peer-reviewed research;
- accepted engineering practice;
- manufacturer-specific data or guidance;
- project-specific evidence;
- operating or failure experience;
- engineering judgement;
- unresolved or conflicting evidence.

These categories should not be silently blended.

A mandatory requirement is not merely another technical opinion.

A manufacturer recommendation is not automatically transferable to every product or service.

A research result is not automatically a project acceptance criterion.

Engineering judgement may be necessary, but it must not be used to disguise missing evidence or bypass mandatory requirements.

> **If an uncertainty is unresolved, it remains unresolved until evidence or a controlled engineering disposition closes it.**

An open question should not silently become an assumption simply because the design process needs to continue.

## 0.7 How to Interpret Requirements and Evidence

The reader should continually distinguish between five different functions.

| Information type | Engineering use |
|---|---|
| **Mandatory requirement** | Establishes a requirement that must be satisfied when applicable |
| **Standardized method or definition** | Provides a controlled technical framework, terminology, test method, or design route |
| **Manufacturer information** | Describes a particular product, system, limitation, procedure, or claimed capability |
| **Engineering evidence / analysis** | Supports a technical decision within defined assumptions and applicability |
| **Engineering judgement** | Resolves or manages decisions where evidence is incomplete, competing, or project-specific |

No single category replaces the others.

A recurring distinction throughout this book is:

> **material qualification ≠ product conformity ≠ system suitability**

A material may be qualified while the finished product is unsuitable.

A product may conform to its specification while the complete installed system is unsuitable for the Design Basis.

A compliant system may still require project-specific verification for temperature, chemistry, loads, joining, installation, operation, or regulatory context.

## 0.8 How to Use Equations, Examples, Tables and Checklists

Engineering tools in this book are intended to support decisions, not replace them.

### Equations

Before using an equation, confirm:

- what physical model or standard it comes from;
- its assumptions;
- its applicability limits;
- required inputs;
- unit consistency;
- whether additional factors or checks are required by the governing standard or Design Basis.

An equation that is mathematically correct can still be applied incorrectly.

### Worked examples

Worked examples demonstrate a method.

They are **not project approvals** and do not establish that the same material, coefficient, pressure, temperature factor, SDR, or acceptance criterion applies to another system.

Where an example contains a critical numerical result, independent recalculation may be required before the result is relied upon in a project decision.

### Tables

Tables may serve different purposes:

- reference;
- comparison;
- screening;
- standards navigation;
- decision support.

The title and surrounding text should make clear which function the table serves.

### Checklists

A checklist item is not complete because the topic was mentioned.

Completion means that the required evidence or engineering disposition has been reviewed and found acceptable.

Where an item is not applicable, **N/A should be justified**.

Where a material issue remains unresolved, the final decision should remain conditional rather than hiding the uncertainty.

## 0.9 Standards Edition and Source Control

Standards change.

A standard number alone is not enough to establish the governing requirement.

Whenever a standards-derived statement materially affects an engineering decision, the engineer should confirm, as applicable:

- standard number;
- edition or publication year;
- amendments and corrigenda;
- scope;
- adopted project or regulatory edition;
- relevant clause, table, annex, or definition;
- whether the document is published, withdrawn, superseded, or still a draft.

A citation in this book does not automatically update when a new edition is published.

Draft documents such as DIS or FDIS should not silently replace a published standard unless the project has explicitly adopted that basis.

Where the book requires clause-level normative accuracy but the authoritative full text has not been verified, the item should remain under a **controlled standards hold** rather than being filled from memory, secondary sources, or an obsolete edition.

## 0.11 How to Read This Book

A newcomer may benefit from reading the foundational chapters sequentially.

An experienced engineer may enter through a specific technical problem and use the book as a reference.

Two early chapters provide important anchors:

- **Chapter 1 — Understanding Industrial Plastic Piping Systems** defines the system-level engineering problem and introduces systems thinking.
- **Chapter 2 — The Engineering Decision Process** provides the recurring decision framework used throughout the book.

Later chapters then develop the technical tools required to support those decisions.

Readers should move between chapters when a problem crosses disciplinary boundaries. A material-selection decision, for example, may require process conditions, long-term strength, joining, installation, hydraulic, mechanical, inspection, and failure-mechanism knowledge.

That is intentional.

The piping system does not respect chapter boundaries.

---

## 0.12 The Goal

The goal is simple:

> **Help engineers make better, more traceable, and more defensible engineering decisions throughout the complete life cycle of industrial plastic piping systems.**

A chapter succeeds only when it improves a real engineering decision in design, construction, operation, inspection, maintenance, modification, or failure investigation.

And a technically correct answer is not yet a complete engineering decision unless its assumptions, evidence, applicability, uncertainty, and required follow-up are visible.
